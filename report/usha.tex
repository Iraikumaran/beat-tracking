\begin{abstract}
This is the progress report of Usha Sundaresan Manivannan for the CSC 475/575 group project DP2\_B on tempo estimation and beat tracking. My part is a neural network beat tracker trained from scratch and a study of downbeat tracking. On the artist-filtered GTZAN test split (\NBeat{} clips) a small multilayer perceptron with peak picking reaches a beat F-measure of \NNF{}; decoding the same network output with madmom's DBN raises it to \NNDBNF{}, against \MadF{} for madmom's full system. For downbeats, a finder based on bass and kick drum onsets reaches F = \DBOracle{} on annotated beats, above a first-beat baseline (\DBFirst{}) but below madmom's bar tracker (\DBMadBar{}). I also report the status of my teammates and our revised plan.
\end{abstract}

\section{Individual Status Report: Usha Sundaresan Manivannan}
\label{sec:me}

\textbf{Objective 1: neural network beat tracker.}
\emph{PI1 (basic, done).} Features and frame labels are built for the 160 training clips (115,860 frames after subsampling negatives) and for every test clip.
\emph{PI2 (basic, partly done).} PyTorch was not available in our first setup, so the first network is an MLP trained with scikit-learn instead of the planned TCN. It stops after 24 epochs. Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows that its activation peaks at most beats, but also at strong off-beat onsets.
\emph{PI3 (expected, done).} On the test split the MLP reaches F = \NNF{}, CMLt = \NNCML{} and AMLt = \NNAML{} (Table~\ref{tab:beats}). Reggae, disco and country are the easiest genres (F above 0.7) and classical the hardest (0.42), as for the other trackers (Figure~\ref{fig:genre}). The low CMLt comes from peak picking, which has no idea of a steady pulse, so the beats drift between on-beat and off-beat positions.
\emph{PI4 (expected, partly done).} madmom's tracker reaches F = \MadF{} and CMLt = \MadCML{}. Passing our own activation through madmom's DBN raises the MLP to F = \NNDBNF{} and CMLt = \NNDBNCML{}, and in the same run it needs \NNDBNTime{}\,s per minute of audio against \MadTime{}\,s for madmom, so a temporal decoder fixes most of the continuity problem; with it, the MLP is more accurate than the DP tracker (\DPF{}) and within 0.05 of madmom. Part of this may be an in-domain advantage, since our network is trained on GTZAN and, as far as we know, madmom's models are not. The remaining gap, largest for jazz and classical, comes from the activation itself, which is what a TCN should improve. The 1D State Space tracker \cite{heydari} is not yet compared.
\emph{PI5 (advanced, not started).} Time-stretch augmentation will be added together with the TCN.

\textbf{Objective 2: downbeat tracking.}
\emph{PI1 (basic, done).} madmom's joint beat and downbeat tracker was run on all test clips and reaches a downbeat F of \DBMadJoint{} from audio alone (Table~\ref{tab:down}).
\emph{PI2 (basic, done).} All downbeat results are evaluated per genre against the annotations (Figure~\ref{fig:dbgenre}).
\emph{PI3 (expected, done).} Running the bar step on annotated beats separates bar errors from beat errors. With annotated beats, madmom's bar tracker reaches \DBMadBar{} and my finder \DBOracle{}. With the MLP's beats my finder falls to \DBNN{}, the same as the first-beat baseline (\DBNNFirst{}), so beat errors dominate: when a third of the beats are wrong, no bar rule can recover the downbeats.
\emph{PI4 (expected, done).} The low-frequency finder of Eq.~(\ref{eq:down}) beats the first-beat baseline (\DBFirst{}) and works well for pop (0.59), classical (0.57) and hip-hop (0.52). It fails for metal (0.07) and disco (0.17), where the kick drum falls on every beat and is weak for blues (0.23). madmom's learned bar model is better in every genre.
\emph{PI5 (advanced, not started).} A downbeat output for the TCN is planned for week 6.

\textbf{What I learned and what did not work.} A frame classifier alone is not a beat tracker; the decoder that enforces a steady pulse matters as much as the network. For downbeats, the bass and kick idea holds for some genres only, and with estimated beats the beat errors hide everything else, so better beats come first.

\section{Team Status}
\label{sec:team}

\textbf{Irai Kumaran Sivanesan} built the autocorrelation tempo estimator. A simple octave decision raises Accuracy1 from \AccOneAC{}\% to \AccOnePrior{}\%, close to librosa (\AccOneLibrosa{}\%) and below madmom (\AccOneMadmom{}\%), while a pulse train stage did not help. A sliding-window version with an octave-folded change detector finds a sudden tempo change within 2\,s in \DetFoldTwo{}\% of spliced clips. GiantSteps remains.

\textbf{Nalluraj Babu} built the dynamic programming tracker of Ellis \cite{ellis}. It reaches F = \DPF{} with its own tempo and \DPOracleF{} with the annotated tempo, as accurate as librosa (\LibF{}). Under 13 degradations it is robust to MP3 and moderate noise and loses most with a 1\,kHz low pass filter (\DegLPOne{}) and pink noise at $-10$\,dB (\DegNoiseMinusTen{}). The 1D State Space comparison and the advanced goals remain.

\section{Revised Plan and Goals}

Our original plan assumed librosa, madmom and PyTorch from the start. Our first setup only had NumPy, SciPy and scikit-learn, so we built everything ourselves and moved the library comparisons later; these are now done for all three methods. Table~\ref{tab:pi} gives the status of every PI and Table~\ref{tab:time} the revised timeline.

\textbf{My goals for the final report.}
\emph{Basic:} keep the MLP and downbeat results as the baseline for the next models.
\emph{Expected:} train a small TCN in PyTorch following \cite{tcn}, decode it with the DBN and compare it with madmom and the 1D State Space tracker \cite{heydari} in accuracy and speed.
\emph{Advanced:} time-stretch augmentation for the TCN, and a second output for downbeats, evaluated against the bar tracker results above.

\textbf{Team goals.} Basic: every method and study complete on GTZAN. Expected: the 1D State Space tracker in all comparisons, and GiantSteps. Advanced: tempo following and automatic tightness for the DP tracker, and tempo change detection on real music.

\section{Conclusion}

The MLP is a reasonable first network: with a proper decoder it is more accurate than the DP tracker and close to madmom, at a fraction of madmom's cost. Downbeats remain hard. The low-frequency rule helps where the kick marks the bar, but a learned model is clearly better, and accurate beats are a precondition. The TCN with a downbeat output is the main step for the final report.
